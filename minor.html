Swachh Bharat Abhiyan

What is Swachh Bharat?

Swachh Bharat Abhiyan (Clean India Mission) is a nationwide cleanliness campaign launched by the Government of India on 2nd October 2014, the birth anniversary of Mahatma Gandhi. The mission was inspired by Gandhi’s dream of a clean and hygienic India, which he considered even more important than independence.

Before Swachh Bharat, cleanliness drives had been launched at different times, but none had such large-scale participation and national importance.

Swachh Bharat is divided into two parts:

Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban) – Focused on 4,041 statutory towns and cities.
Swachh Bharat Mission (Gramin) – Focused on rural sanitation and hygiene.

Key People Behind It
Narendra Modi, the Prime Minister of India, officially launched the Swachh Bharat Mission and is considered the primary initiator and driving force behind it.
It is run under the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (Urban) and the Ministry of Drinking Water and Sanitation (Rural).
Lakhs of government officials, students, NGOs, celebrities, and citizens have contributed to the mission’s success.

Benefits of Swachh Bharat

- Improves overall health and reduces disease outbreaks.
- Boosts tourism by making public spaces more appealing.
- Generates employment opportunities through sanitation work.
- Increases awareness and responsibility among citizens for public cleanliness.
- Reduces pollution in cities and rivers, promoting environmental sustainability.

What Does It Aim to Do?

Swachh Bharat Abhiyan aims to:

- Eliminate open defecation by building toilets and encouraging behavior change.
- Improve solid waste management, including collection, segregation, and disposal.
- Promote awareness on hygiene, cleanliness, and sanitation practices.
- Encourage citizen participation in community cleaning drives.
- Encourage private sector and NGOs to partner in sanitation solutions.

Based on the second aim of Swachh Bharat Abhiyan—improving solid waste management—we developed a project called the Waste Segregation Dustbin.

Why Waste Segregation is Important

Waste segregation involves separating biodegradable (wet) and non-biodegradable (dry) waste. Proper segregation at the source ensures efficient recycling, reduces landfill pressure, and minimizes health hazards. If waste is not segregated:

- Recyclable materials get contaminated and become useless.
- Organic waste ends up in landfills causing methane emissions.
- Waste management becomes expensive and ineffective.
- Spread of diseases increases due to mixed hazardous materials.
- Valuable resources are lost due to inefficient recovery.

Our Project: Waste Segregation Dustbin

As part of supporting the second aim of the Swachh Bharat Abhiyan, which focuses on improving solid waste management—including the collection, segregation, and proper disposal of waste—we have developed an innovative project called the Waste Segregation Dustbin. This project is designed to automate the process of waste separation at the source, making it easier, more efficient, and environmentally friendly.

The Waste Segregation Dustbin works on the principle of identifying and classifying waste into different categories—biodegradable (wet waste), non-biodegradable (dry waste), and in some advanced versions, even metallic waste. It uses sensors such as infrared (IR) sensors, moisture sensors, and metal detectors to detect the type of waste being deposited. Once the type is identified, the system uses a mechanism (like servo motors or conveyor belts) to automatically deposit the waste into the correct bin.
